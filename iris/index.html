What’s in it

A sidebar of Spaces, in a native Mac app.

Spaces

Tabs down the side, a set for each Space.

Each Space keeps its own tabs and, if you like, its own profile, so work logins never meet personal ones. Swipe between Spaces on the trackpad, or press Control and a number. Favorites sit as icons at the top. Pinned tabs return to their page when you close them, and go in folders, folders inside folders if you like. Today’s tabs that you haven’t looked at for 12 hours are put away, and kept for 30 days in the archive.

Links

Glance, Petals and link rules.

A link from a pinned tab to another site opens in Glance, on a sheet of glass over the page, and so does any link you ⇧-click; keep it as a tab, or let it go. A link from another app opens in a Petal, a small window for a quick look, unless a link rule sends that site straight to one of your Spaces.

The engine

WebKit, in a native app.

Pages are drawn by WebKit, the engine Safari uses. Up to four tabs share the window in split view. A tab you haven’t looked at for half an hour gives back its memory, then wakes on the same page, at the same scroll, with its history intact. Iris updates itself: a new version downloads in the background and installs when you quit.

Site styles

Your own type for any site.

Give a site your own CSS, or set it in EB Garamond, and it stays that way every time you visit. Prune clicks away the parts of a page you’d rather not see, and remembers them; Undo puts them back.

Extensions

From the Chrome Web Store.

Chrome extensions run on WebKit’s extension engine, the one Safari’s extensions use. Visit an extension in the Chrome Web Store and Iris shows its own Add button above the page, since Chrome’s won’t work here. Before anything is added, Iris lists what it will be able to do. Extensions that talk to an app on your Mac can.

Passwords

Kept in your Mac’s keychain.

When you sign in, Iris offers to save the password, and next time it offers to fill it, after Touch ID. It fills only on secure pages, and never into a frame hidden inside one. Passwords exported from Dia or Chrome come in all at once from the file.

Privacy

Ads and trackers blocked.

EasyList and EasyPrivacy, compiled for WebKit and refreshed each week. The shield at the end of the address bar holds everything else for the site: blocking, camera, microphone, zoom, sound and its style. Private windows keep nothing on disk and forget everything when they close. Iris has no accounts and sends no telemetry.

On your Mac

Short titles, and a look before you click.

A tab you pin gets a short name, and holding ⇧ over a link shows two sentences about its page. Both are written by Apple Intelligence on your Mac; the page is read without cookies, and nothing is sent anywhere else.

Reading

Reader, Translate, Picture in Picture.

Reader sets an article in EB Garamond, without the page around it. Translate turns a page in another language into yours on your Mac, with Apple’s own translation, and puts the original back with one click. A video floats in Picture in Picture when you switch tabs, and the tab you left keeps its controls in the sidebar.

Asked in plain words, before anything happens.

Switching

The first time it opens, it brings your browser with it.

From Arc

Spaces and all.

Every Space with its pinned tabs, folders and favorites, the Spaces’ icons, and your history.

From Dia

Each profile.

Each profile’s open and pinned tabs, and your history. Your passwords follow from the file Dia exports.

From Chrome, Safari and others

Bookmarks and history.

From Chrome, Brave, Edge and Vivaldi, also your open and pinned tabs, and from Firefox its bookmarks toolbar. macOS keeps some browsers’ files private, so Iris asks for Full Disk Access first. Nothing in the old browser is changed.

Arc and Dia are The Browser Company’s, Chrome is Google’s, Safari is Apple’s, Firefox is Mozilla’s, Brave is Brave Software’s, Edge is Microsoft’s and Vivaldi is Vivaldi Technologies’. Iris isn’t made with or endorsed by any of them; it only reads what they left on your Mac.

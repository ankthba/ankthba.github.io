About

Lagunita is a Garamond in the manner of Jean Jannon: sharp serifs, a contrast that sharpens as it gets heavier, and an italic that leans with a little life in it. Its details come from carved, glyphic lettering: the stems flare slightly where they meet their serifs, the terminals of the f, r, a and c are cut off with a chisel rather than swelling into drops, and the t stands a little taller than usual. Every outer corner is softened a touch.

It was made for screens rather than print. The Text cut, at small optical sizes, has sturdier hairlines, a taller x-height and more room between letters, so it stays clear in paragraphs. The Display cut sharpens the contrast and tightens the fit for headlines. Both sit on one optical size axis, and browsers move along it on their own as the type gets bigger.

The family is two variable fonts, roman and italic, each with a weight axis from 300 to 800 and an optical size axis from 10 to 72. It is built from EB Garamond, by Georg Duffner and Octavio Pardo, and like it is free under the SIL Open Font License.

On a website:

@font-face {
  font-family: "Lagunita";
  src: url("Lagunita-Roman.woff2") format("woff2") tech(variations);
  font-weight: 300 800;
  font-style: normal;
  font-display: swap;
}
@font-face {
  font-family: "Lagunita";
  src: url("Lagunita-Italic.woff2") format("woff2") tech(variations);
  font-weight: 300 800;
  font-style: italic;
  font-display: swap;
}
body { font-family: "Lagunita", Georgia, serif; }

Dawn

Honolulu, 77°

A sky that keeps the hour

Every screen sits on a gradient drawn from the conditions and the sun's position: pink before sunrise here, slate at noon in New York, deep blue over Sydney at three in the morning. It's deepened just enough that white type always reads.

The forecast reads like a page, not a dashboard. The number, a sentence about the day in the app's own voice, then the hours ahead.

"Mostly clear, heading for 82° by noon." The daily brief is composed by careful templating, never an AI.

Noon

New York, 60°

One window on the Mac

Your places in a column, the forecast laid out as a spread, and the radar or Settings in a panel beside it, all on one sky. The temperature sits in the menu bar, and there's a key for everything.

And the iPad, at full width

The Mac's window, under your finger: the forecast on one side, the radar or Settings on the other, and the Mac's keyboard commands when there's a keyboard. In Slide Over it folds back into the iPhone page, and whatever was open stays open.

Dusk

Lisbon, 66°

The sunset, rated before it happens

A score for how good tonight's sunset or tomorrow's sunrise will look, judged from the cloud the forecast promises: high cloud is the canvas, low cloud the wall. Golden hour times come with it, and a heads-up if you want one.

Opened like a book, on iPhone Duo

The forecast holds one page and the sun or the radar the other, meeting at the hinge. On both screens every control stands in the column beside the status bar, with tonight's sunset stood on end at its foot, so the page never runs under a button.

Night

Wherever you are

Current while you sleep

The Apple Watch app and its complications arrive with the iPhone app. The phone hands the wrist the same reading it shows, and when the phone has been quiet a while, the watch fetches its own.

Widgets on the Home and Lock Screens keep up on their own too, so the morning's first glance is the morning's weather, not last night's.

The record

Numbers that agree, and numbers that are right

One reading, everywhere
The main screen, the hourly forecast, your saved places, the widgets, the watch and Siri show the same current temperature.
Checked against an instrument
In the US the nearest weather station corrects the modeled now, so a storm overhead never reads as partly cloudy, and the page says which station it was.
Calibrated where it counts
US highs, lows and rain amounts come from the National Blend of Models, the guidance behind the official forecast, rather than one model running hot.
Clock changes, handled
Sunrise, sunset and every hour after a daylight saving change land on the right minute, and alerts fire in the city's own time.

The index

Everything inside

  1. The radar

    Observed NEXRAD frames and HRRR hours ahead in the US, RainViewer elsewhere, on a timeline you can play or scrub.

  2. The nowcast

    "Rain starting around 3:15," in plain words from 15-minute data, right under the temperature.

  3. The forecast

    Hourly with rain chances, a 72-hour trend you can scrub, and ten days out, each opening hour by hour.

  4. Rain alerts

    A background check warns you before rain starts, even while the app is closed.

  5. Advisories

    National Weather Service watches and warnings in a quiet pill, never a flashing banner.

  6. Live Activity

    An imminent shower on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, with when it arrives and how hard it falls.

  7. The sun

    A sunrise and sunset arc you can drag across the day, and golden hour times.

  8. The moon

    Phase and illumination, computed on the device. The moon doesn't need an API.

  9. The details

    Wind, UV, humidity, pressure, cloud, dew point and precipitation, each opening into a chart.

  10. Air quality

    The US Air Quality Index, with PM2.5, PM10, ozone and nitrogen dioxide behind it.

  11. Widgets

    Home Screen small, medium and large, and the Lock Screen, each pinnable to any saved city.

  12. Siri and Shortcuts

    Ask for the weather and Siri answers from the last forecast, saying how recent it is.

  13. The morning digest

    The daily brief as a notification, at a time you choose.

  14. Two voices

    Every sentence the app composes is written twice: plainly, or with a lighter step.

  15. The share card

    Today's forecast composed as a 4:5 card, the sky and the serif temperature.

  16. Four icons

    One per sky: day, dusk, night and dawn.

  17. Your settings

    Fahrenheit or Celsius, mph or km/h, 12 or 24 hour time, text size, and which cards appear.

  18. Accessibility

    VoiceOver reads every number in context, and Reduce Motion, Contrast and Transparency change the rendering.

Hypnagogia: A Pre-Registered Test for Offline Memory Replay in a Whole-Brain Drosophila Connectome Model

Hypnagogia

Computational Neuroscience · Connectomics · Preprint

Aniketh Bandlamudi · University of Virginia · November 2026

Read the PDF Download Code and data Results viewer Cite

Abstract

In the rat hippocampus, ensembles that fire together during waking fire together again in later sleep, but offline reactivation of a learned ensemble has never been observed in an insect. This paper asks whether a whole-brain leaky integrate-and-fire model of the Drosophila male central brain connectome, 144,209 neurons and 21.9 million connections, built on the model of Shiu et al. (2024), reactivates a learned Kenyon-cell ensemble during simulated sleep.

The reimplementation reproduces the published model on its own data. Two mushroom-body cells are then corrected against direct measurements, adding no parameter: APL becomes a non-spiking, graded-release neuron and DPM becomes inhibitory. An odour memory is encoded by dopamine-gated depression at Kenyon-cell-to-output-neuron synapses and tested against four pre-registered comparisons over 20 seeds.

The test fails. The memory changes offline Kenyon-cell activity in every seed and cuts drive through the learned synapses by a third, but it does not make the trained ensemble reactivate more. A positive control shows that the published model cannot hold a discrete episode; adding spike-frequency adaptation produces episodes, and the memory still contributes nothing. The model’s only plastic synapses sit downstream of the cells being scored, so the negative is a finding about the model, not about the fly.

Results
  • Engine check. The Brian2 reimplementation matches the published model’s own output on FlyWire v630, with per-neuron rate correlations of 0.9997 and 0.9999.
  • The APL correction. Modelling APL as non-spiking, as it was measured to be, takes odour coding from 52.6% of Kenyon cells at 23.0 Hz to 4.5% at 0.79 Hz, against about 6% measured in flies, with nothing else changed.
  • No replay. Only one of the four pre-registered comparisons passes, and the identical sleep run with unlearned weights gives the same reactivation (+0.016, 95% CI −0.011 to +0.044).
  • A tonic memory trace. Offline drive through the learned synapses falls by 32.5%, a 26-fold amplification of a 1.23% weight change, expressed continuously rather than in episodes.
  • Why. The only path from the memory back to the Kenyon cells delivers under 1% of a threshold gap even at 50 Hz, and the corrected APL holds its source neuron far below threshold.
  • With episodes. Adding adaptation, a labelled deviation, gives the model episodes and a realistic sparse code, but in 13 of 20 seeds the ensemble stays on for the whole run, and the memory still contributes nothing (+0.040, 95% CI −0.010 to +0.090).
